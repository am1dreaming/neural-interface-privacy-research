Training an encoder for text does not create the leak: an untrained encoder with the same start leaks as much or more. The information is already in the spectrum, so defences that act only on the embedding miss the raw signal.

02 · More people

Add people,and the spectrum still names them.

Closed-set accuracy has to fall as the number of candidates grows; the question is whether it stays above 1/n. It does, at every size, for every representation. H2 confirmed in 10 of 10 tests.

Encoder trained on 20 development people; attacker tested on people it never saw. 10 seeds, 95% CI. Seeds are not independent replicates, so intervals are a lower bound. On EEGMMIDB the encoder target is the run type, not text.

03 · Architectures

Four encoders,one result.

All four 32-d encoders leak above chance on new ZuCo people. H4 confirmed in 8 of 8 tests. Accuracy overstates the open-set risk: the CNN reaches 0.79, yet its verification EER is only 0.395.

Attacker accuracy on 4 new people · chance 0.25

Trained minus untrained (same architecture and initial weights) is exploratory, not a pre-registered test. H4 was not blind: a short probe run preceded the pre-registration. The ranking is not a claim about architectures in general.

04 · Where it comes from

No single band carries it.All of them do.

Any one frequency group alone names most people; removing any one barely matters. Normalising amplitude per recording costs about 11 points, so part of the fingerprint is contact and cap fit, but most of it is the shape of the spectrum.

Linear attacker, 84 people · chance 0.012
Single channels: +0.29 to +0.41 over chance Strongest: Iz, Oz, PO3, C4, O1 Weakest region: frontal

Exploratory, 5 seeds. At 80 Hz the “gamma” group is capped near 36 Hz, so it is upper beta, which can include muscle activity. The ablation does not separate brain from muscle or from recording conditions.

05 · The one-shot test

Twenty-two people, never seen,evaluated once.

Code and plan were committed before this run (d6587c0). The encoder trained on 84 development people; the attacker met 22 held-out people. No reruns.

Linear attacker, chance 1/22. Privacy side only: this dataset has no text, so a defence that wipes the embedding would look best here. Its price for a decoder is untested.

06 · Reading it honestly

What this shows,and what it does not.

Threat model

An attacker who holds reference recordings of a group and receives raw EEG, its spectral features, or an embedding from any encoder tested here can name the person well above chance. The strongest channel is the spectrum itself. In open-set “same person or not” for unseen people, small encoders sit near chance (EER 0.45–0.50), except the CNN on ZuCo.

What did not work

    Limits

      Timing, stated plainly. The pre-registration was written on 5 November 2026, before the 10-seed runs for H2, H4 and the ablation, and its hypotheses were not edited afterwards. Its first commit came after those runs, so for H2 and H4 the order rests on a local file time. Only the held-out family (H1, H3) ran provably after the commit.
